A Life in Time.

A chronological record of Jeff Neumann's life and practice. Documented facts are marked as such. Gaps are named, not papered over. This page will be updated as oral history sessions continue.

About This Page

This is a working document. The 1970s section is based on documented oral history. The 1980s through 2010s require additional sessions before they can be responsibly published. Dashed placeholders mark what is known to be missing. Nothing here is invented.

1950s

Born

Jeffrey Francis Stanley Neumann was born in 1955 in Cleveland, Ohio.

  • 1955 — Born, Cleveland, Ohio.
Oral history needed

Early childhood. Earliest memory. When and how making things began — before it was understood as art. Family context: parents, siblings, neighborhood. Cleveland in the mid-1950s.

1960s

Childhood

Jeff grew up in Cleveland. He was a maker from early childhood — the specific conditions and early evidence of this have not yet been documented.

Oral history needed

Elementary school years. When did making things first feel like something important? Any early teachers or influences? What did the work look like before art school? Family circumstances and neighborhood through the 1960s.

1970s

Art School. First Sale. First Lost Work.

Jeff attended the Cleveland Institute of Art in the late 1970s. He wanted to be a fine artist. He made sculpture and mixed-media work.

  • Late 1970s — Enrolled at Cleveland Institute of Art (CIA).
  • Late 1970s — Made a sculptural work from iron gridwork found in a junkyard + prism paper + collaged magazine material. Approximately 38" × 24" × 3–4", hung on a wall. Shown in a student exhibition. Sold. Buyer unknown. Whereabouts unknown. No photograph exists. This is the first documented sale and the first documented lost work.
  • 1974 — Earliest work now in the archive. See the 1970s →

"Wanted to be a fine artist."

Oral history needed

What was CIA like? Who were the teachers and peers? What work was Jeff making before the sculptural piece that sold? What did it feel like when that piece sold? What happened after graduation — did he continue trying to establish a fine art career?

1980s

This Decade Is Not Yet Documented

Oral history needed — priority decade

The 1980s are almost entirely absent from the oral history record. This is the decade that followed art school — the period when career, family, and practice all had to find their shape. Key questions: What work did Jeff do professionally in the 1980s? Was he working as a designer? Was he still making art? Did he show work publicly? What was happening in his personal life? This decade requires dedicated oral history sessions before it can be documented.

Archive note

Works from the 1980s are present in the archive. They were made. The context around them — what was happening in Jeff's life while they were being made — has not been captured. See the 1980s works →

1990s

Children Growing. Returning to the Work.

When the children were heading toward college, Jeff thought making things might be a way to earn some extra money. That plan didn't produce income. He kept making things anyway.

"Ha Ha Ha — 30 odd years ago and still making artwork."

Oral history needed

What was Jeff's professional work in the 1990s — was he working as a designer? What were the family circumstances? What specifically prompted the thought that art might earn income? What happened when it didn't? What did the work look like in this period — did it change from the 1980s?

2000s

The Loss.

Water damaged most of what Jeff had made to that point. An estimated five hundred to one thousand works were lost. Jeff stood on the sidewalk outside a friend's building and watched them from approximately six feet away, waiting for the garbage men to take them.

  • Early 2000s — Water damage. Estimated 500–1,000 works lost. Cannot be recovered.

"Sad. Enough."

Oral history needed — most urgent gap

The exact year of the water damage has not been pinned down — "early 2000s" is the current best estimate. Specific works lost: what were they? What subject matter, scale, and medium? Which did Jeff consider his best? One lost work is specifically known to have been a full-scale interpretation of Picasso's Guernica (approximately 11 × 25 feet), made in his studio, never photographed. What happened after the loss — did the practice change? See also: Lost Works → and Six Feet Away →

2010s

This Decade Is Not Yet Documented

Oral history needed

The 2010s are not yet in the oral history record. This decade likely contains significant personal and professional events that have not been captured. It represents an approximately ten-year gap between the known early-2000s loss and the archive-building period of the early 2020s. Works from this decade are present in the archive — context is missing. See the 2010s works →

2020s

Building the Archive. The Collaboration.

Jeff built the archive over several years — alone, digitizing and cataloging more than 1,000 works. He described the process as taking a "fucking long time" with "moments of doubt in the middle."

At approximately the same time, he began incorporating his grandchildren's drawings into new work. The boundary between their contribution and his has become unclear — a condition Jeff describes as right.

  • ~2021–2024 — Built jfsn.com. Cataloged 1,084 works spanning 1974–present.
  • ~2022–present — Incorporating grandchildren's drawings into collaborative works. See the collaboration →
  • 2026 — Oral history sessions begin. Stories, context, and reasons captured for the first time.
  • 2026 — Archive currently contains 1,084 works. Still making.

"Getting hard to tell where they end and I begin. I think that's right."

Oral history needed — ongoing

What does a current day of making look like? Where does Jeff work? What time of day? What does he reach for? The archive documents what was made — not what the practice looks like right now, at 71, still in motion.
