# VISITOR-OBSERVATION-STUDY.md

*Experience Studio Phase Two · Governing methodology document.*
*Completed: 2026-07-01. Do not revise before completing the first round of sessions.*

---

## Research Objective

To observe, without intervention, whether and when a visitor's relationship to the Archive shifts from encountering individual works to encountering something larger — a duration, a condition, a life of making.

This study does not evaluate whether visitors use the Archive correctly. It attempts to identify the moment, if it exists, when the experience of the Archive changes in kind rather than in degree. That moment may not arrive for every visitor. The absence of the moment is as informative as its presence.

---

## Success Criteria

The study is successful if, after three to five sessions, the facilitator can answer the following with reference to direct observation rather than inference:

- In which visitors, if any, did the shift appear to occur?
- At what point in the visit — what page, what position in the grid, what experience — did observable evidence of the shift appear?
- What preceded the shift? What was the visitor doing, and for how long, before it became visible?
- What, if anything, interrupted a shift that appeared to be building?
- In visitors for whom the shift did not occur, what characterized the visit differently?

A study that concludes the shift was not observed in any visitor is successful. That finding is evidence.

---

## Participant Profile

**Who they are**

People who did not know the archive existed before this session. Not designers, not museum professionals, not people with existing relationships to Jeff's work. The ideal participant is someone who has a genuine relationship with made things — a reader, a maker, a person who has spent time with an art collection — but no prior reason to care about this particular archive.

Avoid: people who are inclined to perform appreciation. Avoid: people who will feel pressure to have a response. The visitor should feel genuinely free to be bored, confused, or indifferent.

**What they should know before arriving**

Only this: they are going to spend time with an archive of one person's artwork. No other context. The facilitator does not describe the archive, explain its scope, or characterize what they are about to see.

**Session length**

Unstructured, but the facilitator should protect a minimum of twenty minutes of uninterrupted Archive time before any questions are asked. Some visitors will naturally spend longer. Follow their pace.

## Observation Protocol

**Device**

The primary observation device is a desktop or laptop computer with a browser window open to jfsn.com/archive.html. This ensures the sidebar filter panel is visible alongside the grid from the moment the page loads — the desktop experience the study was designed around. If another device is used, record the device type on the Observation Worksheet, and treat those sessions as a separate condition when synthesizing results.

This round observes only the desktop/laptop condition. It produces no evidence about the mobile experience — the site's primary test target per CLAUDE.md — and its findings should not be generalized there without a separate study.

**What the facilitator does**

Sits to the side or slightly behind the visitor, out of the visitor's direct sightline. Takes notes. Says nothing unless a question moment arises (see Permitted Questions below). Does not react to what the visitor does — no nodding, no sounds of acknowledgment.

Before the session begins, the facilitator says the following and nothing more:

> *"I'm going to ask you to spend some time with this. There's nothing you need to do or find. I'll be quiet while you're in it. I may ask you something afterward."*

The facilitator then opens the Archive and steps back.

**What the facilitator does not do**

- Does not explain the archive.
- Does not explain what 1,084 means.
- Does not describe the chromatic river or any other element.
- Does not prompt the visitor to scroll further, explore more, or stay longer.
- Does not react when the visitor stops, lingers, or leaves a page.
- Does not correct the visitor's navigation.
- Does not tell the visitor they have found something important.
- Does not express that the visit has gone well or poorly.

**What the facilitator is watching for**

Changes in the visitor's pace. The difference between scanning and stopping. Extended contact with a single work or element. Any moment when the visitor appears to have stopped navigating and started receiving. Physical stillness. A change in the quality of attention — not its direction but its register.

These are not findings yet. They are the raw material of observation.

## Permitted Questions

**Moment one: natural pause**

If the visitor pauses — stops scrolling, stops clicking, appears to be still in the Archive without navigating — the facilitator waits. If the pause extends beyond approximately thirty seconds, the facilitator may quietly ask one question. After the answer, the facilitator returns to silence.

Choose one only:
- *"What's happening for you right now?"*
- *"What are you seeing?"*

If the visitor falls silent mid-answer: *"Take your time."*

**Moment two: the visitor moves to leave the Archive**

When the visitor appears to be finishing, ask the exit questions before they have fully disengaged. Not after. The experience is still present.

**Moment three: transition between pages**

If the visitor navigates from the Archive to another page, note the moment and what preceded it. Do not interrupt the transition. Observe what they do on the new page.

Do not manufacture moments. If the visitor moves through the Archive without pausing, do not create a pause by asking a question. The visit is the observation.

---

## Exit Questions

Ask in sequence. Stop after any answer that is substantive.

**If the visitor asks what they should do:** *"Whatever you'd naturally do."*
**If they ask whether there's something specific to find:** *"No."*

1. *"What did you encounter in there?"* — wait for the full answer.
2. *"Was there a moment when it felt different from how it felt at the beginning?"*
3a. If yes: *"Can you describe what changed?"* then *"When did that happen — do you remember where you were in the archive?"*
3b. If no: *"What did it feel like throughout?"*
4. Clear any active filters and scroll to the top of the Archive. Show the visitor the chromatic river. Ask: *"What do you make of this?"* — wait. Do not explain it regardless of their answer.

**Reserve question** — use only once, across all sessions, if a visitor clearly experienced something but cannot find language for it:

*"If you were describing this archive to someone who couldn't visit it, what would you say it is?"*

Record whether this question was used on the Post-Session Synthesis Form.

## Behaviors to Observe Silently

Record without interpretation during the session. Interpretation belongs in the Post-Session Synthesis Form.

**Pace** (use these anchors for consistency across sessions)
- Fast: continuous scroll, no pauses
- Medium: intermittent scroll with brief hesitations
- Slow: frequent stops, sustained attention on individual cards
- Stopped: no movement for 10 or more seconds

**Point of attention**
- Individual work (thumbnail scale)
- Caption or metadata
- Grid as a field (appears to be looking at many works at once rather than one at a time)
- Chromatic river
- Sidebar filters
- Sort controls
- Load More button

**Interactions**
- Filters applied: which, when, in what sequence
- Click-through to individual artwork pages: which works, when in the visit
- Click on chromatic river: yes or no, when
- Load More: clicked or not, how many times
- Time before first filter interaction

**Physical signals**
- Forward lean or backward lean
- Stillness (absence of scrolling or clicking)
- Moment of looking away from screen and then returning
- Any audible response (not its content — simply that one occurred)

**Navigation exits**
- First page the visitor navigates away from the Archive to
- Whether they return to the Archive from that page

## Distinguishing Observation from Interpretation

**The discipline**

During the session: record only what happened. Not what it might mean. Not what it suggests.

After the session: separate notes into two categories before synthesis.

| Observation | Inference |
|---|---|
| Visitor stopped scrolling for 40 seconds at approximately card 60. | The visitor may have entered a receiving state. |
| Visitor applied "1990s" filter at 3 minutes. | The visitor may have been in a deciding state at that point. |
| Visitor said "I didn't realize there was so much." | The quantity may have registered differently than the work. |
| Visitor did not click Load More. | Unknown — may indicate satisfaction, fatigue, or something else. |

Do not collapse the two categories. The observation is durable. The inference is provisional.

**The test for an observation**

An observation passes if another person present in the room would have recorded the same thing. If it requires knowledge of the visitor's internal state, it is an inference. If it requires knowledge of what the Archive is trying to do, it is an inference. Record it anyway — in the inference column.

## Synthesizing Results After Multiple Sessions

**After each session:** complete the notes template. Write one sentence: *What happened in this session that I did not expect?* Answer with what surprised you, not with what confirmed your hypotheses.

**After all sessions:** lay the chronological logs side by side. Look for patterns in behavior first, then in language.

Patterns to look for:
- Is there a consistent point — a card count, an elapsed time — where pace changes? Or different every time?
- Do visitors who filter early visit differently than visitors who do not?
- What language do visitors use for the whole versus individual works? Count words describing objects versus words describing duration, accumulation, or continuity.
- How many visitors mention the chromatic river without being asked?
- How many visitors, when shown the river, describe it in terms of time versus navigation?

**The synthesis question**

After reviewing all sessions, ask only this:

*In which visitors, at what moment, and under what conditions did the experience of the Archive appear to change in kind rather than in degree?*

If the answer is never, in none of the visitors — that is the finding. It does not mean the Archive failed. It means the conditions for the transformation have not yet been observed in this study, and the Experience Studio has more work to do.

If the answer is in some visitors, at approximately this point, after approximately this duration, preceded by approximately these conditions — that is evidence. Not proof. Evidence.

If sessions disagree with each other — the shift appears under one set of conditions in some visitors and is absent under similar conditions in others — that disagreement is itself data, not noise to be resolved by outvoting it. Report it as disagreement. Do not average it into a false consensus, and do not default to whichever finding was already expected.

The purpose of evidence is not to confirm what we already believe. It is to make the next decision less wrong than it would have been without it.
