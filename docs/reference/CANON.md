# The Canon of the JFSN Archive

*The map of the archive's governing ideas.*
*Read this second, immediately after CONSTITUTION.md, before any other document.*
*This document orders what governs; it does not govern in their place. On questions of substance it defers entirely to the documents it maps. Where it and CONSTITUTION.md differ, the Constitution governs.*
*Version 1.0 — 2026-07-07.*

---

## 1. Why the Canon Exists

This archive was built by one person and will pass to people who never met him. Over its life it will be redesigned many times, by many hands, in technologies that do not yet exist. Each of those redesigns will face the same danger: to preserve what does not matter and discard what does, because the reasons were never written down where the next custodian would look.

The Constitution states what must remain true. The other governing documents develop and apply it — one defines how the archive's permanent experiences coexist, others define a single experience and how it is built. But a set of documents is not self-organizing. A custodian arriving cold needs to know which to read, in what order, which one wins when two seem to disagree, and which parts they are free to change. Without that, well-meaning effort drifts: each hand keeps a different thing, and over enough hands the archive becomes something its maker would not recognize.

The Canon is the map that prevents this. It governs nothing on its own. It orders what governs, so that the archive's own ideas do not drift apart from one another over time. Its only purpose is coherence across decades.

## 2. Authority Order

Authority in this archive flows in one direction. When any two layers appear to conflict, the higher layer governs, without exception.

```
        The Constitution        — what must remain true
                ↓
        The Canon (this map)     — which document governs, and where each applies
                ↓
        Experience Orchestration — how the permanent experiences coexist as one
                ↓
        Element Philosophy       — what a single experience is, and means
                ↓
        Production Specification — how that experience is faithfully built
                ↓
        Implementation           — the actual pages of any given era
                ↓
        Technology               — the platform beneath them
```

**Why authority flows in this order.** Each layer exists to serve the one above it and may be replaced without loss so long as it keeps faith with that layer. The higher a layer sits, the slower it changes and the more it protects; the lower it sits, the more freely it may be remade. Truth changes least, and so sits at the top. Technology changes most, and so sits at the bottom. A conflict is always resolved in favor of the higher layer, because the higher layer is the *reason the lower one exists* — an implementation serves a specification, a specification serves a philosophy, a philosophy serves the orchestration, the orchestration serves the Constitution, and the Constitution serves Jeff. To let a lower layer overrule a higher one is to let a means overrule its end.

**The Canon's own place.** This document sits directly beneath the Constitution as a *procedural* authority, not a substantive one. It decides which document governs and where each applies; it never decides what is true or how an experience should feel. On any question of substance it is silent and defers downward to the proper document. Its authority is only over order.

**The Foundations record stands beside this order, not within it.** The Experience Studio Foundations is the history that produced everything below the Constitution — the record of what was discovered and why. It is read for understanding, never obeyed as instruction. Where it conflicts with any operative document above, the operative document governs. It is origin, not law.

**Where the River and the Wall are defined.** Two of the three permanent experiences do not yet have their own philosophy and specification documents. Until and unless such documents are written, their governing definition is the Experience Orchestration together with their living implementation. A custodian is not required to write those documents; the absence is deliberate and sufficient.

## 3. Permanent vs Evolvable

The single most important distinction in this archive: **permanence attaches to a perceptual role, never to the mechanism that realizes it.**

**What must remain — permanent:**
- The Constitution's non-negotiables, in full — above all that the work is shown honestly and that nothing is ever fabricated.
- The perceptual role of each canonical experience: the whole felt as time, the whole felt as mass, recurrence confirmed to one who returns. These roles are the archive's identity. They survive every redesign.
- The principles that the roles depend on: meaning is earned and never announced; presence precedes interpretation; the visitor is rewarded for attention, never made to pay it.

**What may evolve:**
- *Implementation* — how any role is realized. An experience may be rebuilt from nothing and remain canonical, provided its perceptual role is intact.
- *Technology* — the platform, the formats, the methods. All of it is replaceable and will be replaced.
- *Editorial wording* — the exact words, provided they keep the archive's voice and never soften its truth.
- *Interaction mechanics* — thresholds, timings, transitions, affordances, and other tunable detail, provided they still serve the role.

**The rule that follows:** an element is violated only when its role is lost, not when its mechanism changes. A custodian who rebuilds an experience in a new medium and keeps its role has honored the canon completely. A custodian who preserves the old mechanism but lets the role decay has broken it, however faithful the surface appears. Judge by the role. Everything beneath the role is yours to remake.

Be warned of one trap: some changes presented as mere mechanics are in truth decisions about Jeff's work. Altering which of his obsessions an experience recognizes, or how his words are framed, is a curatorial act even when it looks like a setting. Such changes are made deliberately, and recorded, never adjusted casually.

## 4. Canonical Elements

Four elements are permanent: one law and three experiences. Their *roles* are defined here. Their design lives in their own documents and is not restated.

**The Constitution — the law.**
*Responsible for:* what must remain true across every redesign — the honesty of the work, the earning of meaning, the openness of the record.
*Never responsible for:* how anything is built, sequenced, or experienced. It sets no timing and draws no layout.
*Why it exists:* so the archive's truth outlives everyone who tends it, including its maker.

**The Chromatic River — the whole, as time.**
*Responsible for:* letting a visitor feel the full span of the practice as one continuous body before they have understood any single part.
*Never responsible for:* presenting a work in detail, claiming that a shape recurs, or delivering the whole as overwhelming mass. Those belong to other elements.
*Why it exists:* so a visitor feels *how long* before they ask *what* — the register of the archive established before the first choice. (It may be named to visitors.)

**The Wall — the whole, as mass.**
*Responsible for:* presenting the entire body of work at once, late, as the revelation that the works are not a collection but an act.
*Never responsible for:* explaining itself, setting the opening register, or isolating any single thread. It arrives after the visitor is ready for it, never before.
*Why it exists:* so the transformation from seeing objects to feeling a lifetime of making can complete, which no single work and no summary can accomplish. (It may be named to visitors.)

**The Same Hand — recurrence, confirmed.**
*Responsible for:* confirming, to a visitor who has returned to a shape Jeff returned to, that the work before them is one instance of a sustained obsession.
*Never responsible for:* announcing itself, demanding attention, presenting the whole, or being named. It only ever confirms what a visitor has already begun to notice.
*Why it exists:* so a visitor *feels* the compulsion behind the practice rather than being told of it — the archive's thesis made bodily. (It is never named to visitors. This asymmetry with the River and the Wall is deliberate: those are places a visitor goes; this is a recognition a visitor has.)

## 5. Custodial Principles

How a custodian should decide — not how they should build.

- **Read down to act; defer up to resolve.** To do the work, read from the Constitution downward until you reach the layer you are changing. To settle a disagreement, defer upward until one document plainly governs. Most apparent conflicts are simply a lower document being read past its authority; check the order before you touch anything.
- **Change the lowest layer that solves the problem.** Never reach higher than the trouble requires. A rendering fault is fixed in implementation, not in philosophy. Reaching up to solve a problem that lived below is how canons are damaged.
- **Protect roles; replace mechanisms without hesitation.** The mechanism is not precious. The role is. Rebuild freely so long as the role survives.
- **Prefer leaving it alone.** This archive is closer to finished than to unfinished. The urge to justify one's tenure by producing change is the most common way a settled thing is worn down. Restraint here is not neglect; it is the care this particular archive most requires.
- **Let the guiding question rule.** *Will this help a future grandchild understand Jeff and his life better?* Every domain-specific test in the lower documents is only an expression of this one. When the expressions seem to differ, return to the question itself; it is supreme.
- **Distinguish discovery from invention.** The right change is almost always something the archive was already reaching toward, recognized — not something new imposed. When a proposal feels like invention, suspect it.

## 6. Amendment Process

The canon is amended almost never. This is not caution; it is the point. A map that is redrawn often is not a map.

**The lower documents may be revised** within their authority — a specification refined, an implementation rebuilt, wording corrected — without amending the canon at all, because the Authority Order already permits the layers beneath to change. Most work touches nothing here.

**This map and the Constitution are amended only on the rarest evidence.** Three kinds, and no others:
- A **truth about Jeff or the work** is newly established by him or by verified testimony — a fact the archive must now hold honestly.
- A **new permanent experience is discovered** — not invented — and proven, over time and not in a single session, to be inseparable from the archive in the way the existing three are. The burden of proof is high and the default answer is no.
- A **structural ambiguity in the order itself** is shown, by repeated real custodial conflict, to produce genuinely divergent decisions — and only the ordering, not the substance, is clarified.

**What never justifies an amendment:** a new technology; a visual refresh; a fashion of the day; a single custodian's preference; the wish to improve, to modernize, or to leave a mark; or the completion of a redesign. Every one of these is implementation, and implementation changes without touching the canon.

**The test for any amendment** is severe by design: *could you not keep faith with Jeff without this change?* If the archive can remain true, whole, and open without it, do not make it. When an amendment is genuinely warranted, it is made deliberately, dated, and recorded with its reason, so that the next custodian inherits not only the change but the evidence that compelled it.

## 7. Closing — The End of the Experience Studio

The Experience Studio is complete.

Its work was never to decorate the archive but to discover what the archive already was, and to write it down where it could not be lost. That is done. The Constitution holds the truth. This Canon holds the order. The experiences are defined, and the one that needed building is specified to the last honest detail. There is no further philosophy to find, because the archive's philosophy was only ever the faithful description of one man's practice, and it has now been described.

What remains is not more design. It is stewardship and implementation — the keeping of what has been settled, and its patient rebuilding as the years require. Those are quieter labors than design, and they are the only labors this archive now needs. A custodian who feels the pull to reopen the studio, to invent one more experience, to refresh what is already true, should read this document again and then close it: the temptation to keep designing is exactly the drift the canon exists to prevent.

The design program is finished. The archive is not, and never will be — it stays open because the making stayed open, and one day, when the making finally stops, the record will still say *present*, because a life that long does not end on the day its hand goes still.

Let the studio close. Let the keeping begin.
