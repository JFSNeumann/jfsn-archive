# JFSN Legacy Session Checkpoint — 2026-06-09

## What Was Learned Today

### Creative Identity

Jeff always thought he was good at art.

Making art became a way of life.

The desire to create persisted regardless of money, recognition, career, or circumstances.

---

### Found Materials

Found-object work was not originally an artistic philosophy.

It began because there was little money for materials.

Over time, necessity became identity.

Materials remembered today include:

- CDs
- Prisma paper
- tape

There were many others, but some are difficult to remember now.

---

### Loss

The archive is only a fragment of the total body of work.

More than half of Jeff's life's work may have been lost.

Many pieces disappeared before ever being photographed.

Important quote:

*"fragment / 100s lost without taking a picture first"*

---

### The Lost Guernica

One of the most significant lost works:

A full-scale interpretation of Picasso's Guernica approximately 11 × 25 feet.

Destroyed in the water damage.

Never photographed.

Exists only in memory.

This may be one of the most important missing works in the entire archive.

---

### Anselm Kiefer

The strongest new insight from today:

When asked what he would make if money were no object, Jeff immediately pointed toward Anselm Kiefer.

Not because of one specific feature.

When asked whether it was scale, materials, or subject matter — the answer was:

*"everything"*

Important implication:

The found objects and limited resources were reality. They were not necessarily the original vision. The imagination always operated at a much larger scale.

The archive is not the whole story. The archive is what survived.
