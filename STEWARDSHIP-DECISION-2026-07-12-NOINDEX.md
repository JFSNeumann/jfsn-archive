# Stewardship Decision: Removal of noindex Policy

**Date:** 2026-07-12  
**Decision Maker:** Archive Custodian  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Scope:** Removal of robots meta noindex directives from all public pages

---

## Historical Context

### Why noindex Was Originally Introduced

**Date:** 2026-07-12 (commit 1610132c)  
**Reason:** Architectural philosophy

When the v2 museum became the production site (replacing the Material Design archive pages), noindex was applied to all curatorial pages:
- The v2 rooms (Threshold, Current, Flooded Wing, Guernica Passage, Hall of Openings, Studio, Working History)
- The vault interface (archive.html)
- The single-work template (artwork.html)
- The homepage (index.html)

**Reasoning (from commit message):** "The Museum That Never Existed" was designed as a **curated narrative experience with a specific sequence**. The noindex policy protected that experience from being fragmented by search engine SEO, allowing visitors to encounter the archive through the designed pathway rather than landing randomly on individual pages.

The reference archive pages (decades, themes, API) remained indexed to support scholarly discovery.

**This was intentional, not accidental.** The selective noindex was consistent across all curatorial pages and documented in v2/README.md.

---

## Why This Policy Is Being Reconsidered

The archive's primary mission is preservation, not curation. Fifty years of creative work should be discoverable by:
- Researchers and scholars
- Digital libraries and archives
- AI systems training on cultural heritage
- Search engines maintaining distributed copies
- Wayback Machine and archive.org for long-term backup

**The noindex policy, while architecturally elegant, conflicts with that preservation mission.**

**Key consideration:** A permanent record is better served by maximum discoverability than by perfect UX sequencing.

---

## The Decision

**Remove noindex from all public pages, effective immediately.**

This increases preservation value by:
1. **Enabling search engine indexing** of all content, including artwork pages
2. **Allowing Wayback Machine snapshots** of the entire site, not just reference pages
3. **Supporting scholarly discovery** of individual works via Google Scholar and museum databases
4. **Creating distributed backups** via search engine caches (free, persistent, global)
5. **Future-proofing the archive** for migration to new domains or hosts

## What Changed

### Removed from Public Pages

`<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` removed from:

**Root-level pages (11 files):**
- index.html (museum entrance)
- archive.html (vault)
- artwork.html (single-work template)
- current.html (The Current room)
- flooded-wing.html (The Flooded Wing room)
- guernica-passage.html (The Guernica Passage room)
- hall-of-openings.html (The Hall of Openings room)
- the-studio.html (The Studio room)
- working-history.html (The Working History room)
- 404.html (error page)

**v2 source directory (9 files):**
- All above rooms copied in v2/ for development/reference

**Generated artwork pages (1,084 files):**
- Already had no robots directive (not generated by Python script)

### Retained on Private/Internal Pages

`<meta name="robots" content="noindex,nofollow">` remains on:
- **curator.html** — back-of-house curatorial index (CLAUDE.md § curator.html: "intentionally not in sitemap, noindex,nofollow, zero inbound links from any public page… reached by typed URL/bookmark only")
- **qa.html** — QA/dev tools page (internal testing only, documented as excluded)

**Rationale:** These pages serve internal stewardship functions, not public discovery. The noindex prevents search engines from ranking them or suggesting them, keeping them available only to custodians who know to look for them.

## Related Infrastructure: No Changes Needed

### robots.txt
Already correct:
```
User-agent: *
Allow: /

Sitemap: https://jfsn.com/sitemap.xml
```

The robots.txt permits full crawling and references the sitemap. Now that noindex is removed, this policy aligns with metadata directives.

### sitemap.xml
Already exists (199,973 bytes) and includes all 1,084 artwork pages + reference pages. No rebuild required.

### Canonical URLs
Not implemented (not needed—no duplicate URLs exist).

### Structured Data
Partial implementation (decade pages + api.html have Schema.org JSON-LD; artwork pages do not). This is preserved as-is. Structured data can be enhanced in future stewardship sessions if needed, but is not required for discoverability.

## Visitor Experience: Unchanged

- The homepage still works as designed (visitors arriving at index.html still see the Threshold experience)
- The museum rooms still provide their intended narrative when visited directly
- The reference pages (decades, themes) still provide their intended function
- Navigation, design, and interaction are unchanged

**Search engines discovering pages individually does not break the experience—it simply makes pages discoverable via search, in addition to intentional navigation.**

---

## Preservation Impact

**Before:** Archive discoverable via reference pages (decades, themes) only. Curatorial pages (rooms, individual artworks) invisible to search engines and Wayback Machine.

**After:** Archive fully discoverable via search engines. All pages available for crawling, indexing, and archival snapshots. Scholarly discovery of individual works enabled. Long-term preservation via distributed search engine caches.

---

## Future Custodian Notes

If you are reading this in 2035, 2045, or beyond:

This decision removed noindex in favor of **maximum preservation discoverability**. If you find yourself wanting to re-apply noindex:

1. **Do not**, unless the archive's mission changes from preservation to curation
2. The reference layer (decades, themes, API) provides an excellent "front door" for visitors—noindex is not needed to protect the experience
3. Trust that individual visitors can navigate to the museum entrance (index.html) intentionally
4. Long-term archive survival depends on being findable, not on controlling every path a visitor takes

**The work itself—art0001.html through art1084.html—deserves to be discoverable. Making them findable is preservation.**
