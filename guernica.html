Guernica

232 works · 1990–2020

Jeff read a book about how Picasso made Guernica. He considered it the most important painting of the twentieth century — the one that showed what painting could do when history demanded something of it. What drew him to it as a structure for his own work: "Nice and big. Used it as a direction, a structure to work against."

That was sometime around 1990. The series ran for thirty years. 232 works. The Guernica composition — warplanes, compressed bodies, fractured geometry — became a scaffold he could build anything on. The specific materials changed decade by decade: prism paper, compact discs as halos, vinyl, target forms, found objects from whatever was available. The structure underneath stayed. He is still reworking old Guernica pieces today.

Somewhere in this period — the decade is not documented — he made a full-scale interpretation of Picasso's painting in his basement studio. Approximately 11 × 25 feet, built on flexible material so it could be rolled and stored. It lived in the basement for years. No one else ever saw it. Later moved to storage. Lost to water damage. Never photographed. When asked, after thirty years of working with the composition, why he finally made something at that full scale: "It was about time." That work is not in this archive.
