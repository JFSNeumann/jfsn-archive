## What Should Not Be Revisited Before Running Sessions

The following questions were asked and answered during the design phase. Reopening them before gathering observation evidence would be the wrong order.

- Whether the progress bar should be removed. *(It has been removed.)*
- Whether 96 is the right initial page size. *(96 is not the hypothesis. The hypothesis is about the timing of interruption. The number is a starting condition, not a conclusion.)*
- Whether the chromatic river needs a label, a change in scale, or modified interactivity. *(This is precisely what the observation is designed to investigate. Do not design a solution before the evidence is in.)*
- Whether the sidebar, sort controls, or work count require intervention. *(These were evaluated and ranked by confidence. All are lower confidence than the river. Observe first.)*

The Experience Studio's most important finding was that the correct design decision is sometimes no design decision. Returning to these questions before observations are complete would be designing against evidence rather than toward it.

## Guiding Principles for Conducting Sessions

These come from the foundations. They should be held in mind throughout every observation.

**Evidence before change.** The purpose of the observation phase is to improve understanding, not to confirm what we already believe. A session that contradicts current thinking is more valuable than one that confirms it.

**Presence before interpretation.** During a session, record what happens. Interpretation belongs in the Synthesis Form, completed afterward.

**Protect the visitor's natural experience.** The facilitator's silence is not passivity — it is the primary methodological discipline. Any word spoken during a session that was not specified in the protocol is contamination.

**The absence of the transformation is as informative as its presence.** If no visitor in the first round experiences the shift from objects to practice, that finding has implications for the Archive's architecture that no design session has yet produced.

## Order of Documents During a Real Observation

**Before the session:**
1. Facilitator Packet — review the setup, opening script, and question list.

**During the session:**
2. Observation Worksheet — timestamps, behavior log, interaction checkboxes, exact quotations.

**Immediately after the visitor leaves:**
3. Complete the Observation Worksheet from memory where gaps remain.

**Within one hour:**
4. Post-Session Synthesis Form — separate observations, quotations, reflections, and inferences. Answer the required question last.

Do not consult the full Visitor Observation Study during a session. The Facilitator Packet contains everything needed. The Study is the reference document for designing the synthesis, not a live operational guide.

## What Constitutes a Successful First Round

Three to five completed sessions with first-time visitors, each producing:

- A completed Observation Worksheet with a chronological log covering the full visit.
- At least two exact visitor quotations — one from during the visit or at its close, one from the chromatic river question.
- A completed Post-Session Synthesis Form with the four categories kept separate.
- An honest answer to the required question: *What happened in this session that I did not expect?*

A round of sessions in which the transformation was never observed, or in which every visitor filtered immediately and left the receiving state before accumulation could begin, is a successful round. The discipline is to report what happened, not to produce an encouraging finding.

---

## After the First 3–5 Sessions Are Complete

Lay the Observation Worksheets side by side before reading the Synthesis Forms.

Look first at behavior, not language: pace changes, stillness, the timing of first filter interactions, whether Load More was clicked or not. Patterns in behavior are more durable evidence than patterns in language.

Then read the Synthesis Forms together. Note where inferences from different sessions agree and where they conflict. Conflicting inferences are not a problem — they are the data.

Bring the findings back to the Experience Studio using the established method:

1. Observe the existing experience.
2. Identify the perceptual transformation.
3. Evaluate against the foundations.
4. Identify what any proposed response protects, risks, and interrupts, and whether it increases or decreases the visibility of the interface.
5. Recommend exactly one outcome: Implement, Prototype, or Reject.

The observation evidence should drive the next proposal. If the evidence is ambiguous, the next step is more observation, not more design.

---

## On Archive Changes

**No changes should be made to the Archive until the observation evidence from the first round of sessions has been reviewed.**

This applies to the chromatic river, the sidebar, the sort controls, the work count, the page size, and any other element identified during the design phase as a candidate for intervention.

The two existing prototypes — progress bar removal and page size increase — are already in place. They are the conditions under which the first round of observations will occur. Changing anything else before observations are complete would make it impossible to attribute any finding to a specific cause.

The Experience Studio's discipline is that evidence precedes change. The observation phase is how evidence is gathered. Until it is gathered, the Archive should remain exactly as it is.
