Welcome

You've found the personal archive of Jeffrey F. S. Neumann — a working record of fifty years of making. There are 1,084 works here: collage, assemblage, sculpture, painting, and things that don't fit neatly into any of those categories.

This isn't a gallery in the traditional sense. Nothing is for sale here. There are no artist statements, no press releases, no career summaries. What's here is the work — and some of the context that helps it make sense.

Why This Archive Exists

For most of his life, Jeff made things without much documentation. Works were given away, sold, left in storage, or simply forgotten.

Then a friend called. Jeff went to see if he could save anything. He was told to stay away — bed bugs. He saw the work on the curb from about six feet away, waiting for the garbage truck. An estimated 500 to 1,000 pieces. Some of what he considered his best work. Some pieces that exist now only in a single photograph.

"Sad. Enough."

He built this archive alone. It took a long time. "Took a fucking long time. Moments of doubt in the middle." He built it so the work wouldn't disappear twice — and so his family would know what existed, including the pieces that are only in one photograph now.

It's a record. Not a monument, not a brand. A record.

About Jeff

Jeffrey Francis Stanley Neumann was born in 1955 and has lived most of his life in Cleveland, Ohio. He went to the Cleveland Institute of Art intending to become a fine artist. That was the plan.

Life followed a different path. He spent decades working as a product designer and creative director, while making art steadily in the gaps — evenings, weekends, whatever time was left. Art supplies were always too expensive, so the work was built from whatever was available: CDs, computer floppies, keyboards, prism paper, things from the dollar store. "Something that still had a life left in it."

The Guernica series — Picasso's painting used not as homage but as a direction to work against — ran for more than thirty years and produced 232 works. He is still reworking old Guernica pieces today.

For the past several years, he has been incorporating his grandchildren's drawings into new work. "Getting hard to tell where they end and I begin." He considers this right.

He's been retired since around 2020. He still makes things most days.

How To Explore

There's no single way to see this archive. The Archive itself holds the complete record—every work, searchable. But the same body of work can also be read as color in the Chromatic River. Seen whole in the Wall. Mapped for pattern in the Curatorial Map. Narrowed to what Jeff would point to first in Favorites. Or gathered into its own exhibition in the Imagined Museum. Each reveals something the others don't. Start wherever your curiosity leads.

The full archive has everything. The series index groups work by recurring subjects. The decade pages trace how the work changed over time.

If you want Jeff's own shortlist — the works he'd point to first — visit Favorites.

For Researchers

If you're analyzing the archive, building research tools, or incorporating the work into an academic context, the API provides machine-readable access to all 1,084 works: metadata, themes, palettes, and direct image links.

The data is free to use under CC BY 4.0. All years are decade estimates; ~250 works are Photoshop composites (flagged in the data). Browse the API documentation for JSON endpoints, CSV export examples, data accuracy notes, and recommended research workflows.
