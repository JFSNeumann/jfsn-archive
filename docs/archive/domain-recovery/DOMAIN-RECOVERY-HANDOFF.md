# jfsn.com — A Plain-Language Handoff

**Who this is for:** a family member, friend, or future caretaker who needs to look after Jeff Neumann's online archive — especially if Jeff is not available to explain it. You do **not** need to be technical to follow this. It is written to still make sense ten years from now.

**The one-sentence version:** Jeff's life's work is safely backed up in several places and will not be lost — but the web address it lives at, **jfsn.com**, needs a little care to keep, and this document explains how.

---

## What JFSN is

JFSN is the personal archive of **Jeffrey F. S. Neumann** — an artist from Cleveland, Ohio. Over about fifty years he made more than a thousand works: collages, sculptures, and photographs. **1,084 of those works** are gathered, photographed, and described in this archive.

This is not a store and not a business. It is a record of a creative life. Many of Jeff's earlier works — possibly five hundred to a thousand of them — were destroyed years ago by water damage in storage, with no photographs and no list. That loss is the reason this archive exists: so the rest can never simply disappear the same way.

## Why jfsn.com matters

**jfsn.com** is the archive's address on the internet — the name people type, the link in any printed material, the place a grandchild or researcher would go to find Jeff's work. The pictures and writing are safe no matter what (see "Where the archive exists" below). But if the **name jfsn.com** is lost, every link and reference to it breaks, and someone else could even take the name. Keeping jfsn.com is about keeping the archive's permanent, recognizable identity — its front door.

## What has been discovered

In 2026, a careful check of how jfsn.com is set up found the following, all confirmed:

- **Jeff is the legal owner of the domain name.** Official records list the owner as "Jeff Neumann," Ohio. This is the most important fact — it means Jeff (or his family acting for him) has the right to recover it.
- **The domain is held inside a friend's account** at a company called **Gandi** (the "registrar" — the company that manages domain names). The friend has been helping keep it registered. Jeff does not currently have his own login to that account.
- **The name is paid up until March 5, 2027**, and it is **not locked** — meaning it can be moved to Jeff's own account fairly easily once the right person acts.
- **The website is currently hosted at a company called HostGator**, but **no one can log into that HostGator account anymore.** This does not threaten the archive (everything is backed up elsewhere), but it does mean HostGator should eventually be left behind.

## What remains unresolved

Neither of these threatens the artwork. They are about control and tidiness for the long term.

## How recovery should proceed

The goal is simple: **move the domain name jfsn.com into an account that Jeff (or his family) personally controls, then keep it renewed.** In order of preference:

1. **Ask the friend who holds the account.** This is by far the easiest path. Ask them to either move the domain to Jeff's own Gandi account, or give you the domain's "transfer code" so it can be moved. This can often be done in a few days.
2. **If the friend cannot be reached,** contact **Gandi** (the registrar, at gandi.net) and explain that **Jeff Neumann is the registered owner** and needs to recover the domain. Gandi has a process for this. You will need to prove identity (see "What evidence already exists" below). This can take a few weeks.
3. **If Gandi cannot help,** there is a formal complaint process through **ICANN** (the international body for domain names, at icann.org), based on Jeff being the rightful owner. This is a last resort and slower.
4. **Above all, make sure the name is renewed before its expiry date** so it is never lost while recovery is underway.

Once the domain is in Jeff's own account: turn on extra login security (two-factor), turn on automatic renewal, and the front door is secured for the long term. A technically-minded helper can then point the address at any of the backup copies — but that step is not urgent and the artwork is safe in the meantime.
