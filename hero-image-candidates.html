Hall of Openings — Hero Image Candidates

Five finalists for the entrance image. Each candidate is evaluated against: immediacy of presence, representation of the Hall as a whole, curiosity before explanation, mobile responsiveness, and museum-quality restraint. Rank them by your curatorial judgment.

CANDIDATE 1: CURRENT (for reference)
Title
Installation View, Wall of Works
ID · Year · Aspect
art0028 · 2000s (est.) · 400×300

Why it succeeds

  • Balanced proportions — Nearly square 400×300 ratio works equally well on desktop and mobile
  • Full gallery wall — Shows multiple works arranged on a single surface, immediately reads as curation
  • Visual richness — Colorful, patterned, invites exploration
  • Represents the Hall — This is what "installation view, wall of works" looks like

What it communicates first

  • Visual abundance and pattern
  • Intentional curation (works placed deliberately)
  • Aesthetic playfulness (color, composition)
  • A space designed by someone with visual intelligence

Weaknesses

  • Possibly too colorful/busy — Might overshadow the narrative of "imagined"
  • Doesn't signify scale — Doesn't communicate "this is a room" vs. "this is a detail"

Support for principle

Visitor encounters visual evidence of imagined curation before being told "these rooms never held it." The image alone suggests sophistication and intention. Text that follows deepens understanding.

CANDIDATE 2
Title
XXXIII Large-Scale Installation
ID · Year · Aspect
art0827 · 2000s (est.) · 400×289

Why it succeeds

  • Title explicitly promises scale — "Large-Scale Installation" signals that this is a room-sized imagining
  • Good responsive ratio — Nearly square, works on all screens
  • Part of XXXIII series — Links to Guernica, a central practice over 30 years
  • Serious composition — Suggests intentional, curatorial work

What it communicates first

  • Scale and ambition
  • Long-term commitment (XXXIII = series)
  • Museum-quality installation thinking
  • A room you could walk through

Weaknesses

  • May read as more formal/austere than welcoming — Could signal "research project" rather than "gallery experience"
  • Less colorful — Might not draw the eye as immediately

Support for principle

Visitor sees proof of ambitious imagining before learning the premise. The image alone demonstrates that these are thoughtfully-designed spaces, not idle fantasies. Title deepens understanding.

CANDIDATE 3
Title
XXXIII Guernica Installation
ID · Year · Aspect
art0816 · 2000s (est.) · 400×225

Why it succeeds

  • Guernica reference — Central to Jeff's 30-year practice, immediately signals seriousness
  • Reasonable landscape ratio — Not too compressed, works on mobile with minor cropping
  • Balances formality and accessibility — Professional without being cold
  • Clear installation language — "Installation" in title signals imagined space

What it communicates first

  • A specific artistic preoccupation
  • Historical/conceptual depth
  • Museum-quality presentation
  • Work that references art history

Weaknesses

  • Compressed aspect ratio (400×225) — Might feel cramped on mobile, visible detail may be lost
  • Signals a specific series rather than the Hall as a whole — Visitors might assume all 250 are Guernica-related

Support for principle

Visitor encounters a sophisticated, historically-informed imagining before explanation. The image alone communicates artistic rigor. Aspect ratio is the main trade-off for mobile.

CANDIDATE 4
Title
Untitled (Installation with Bicycles)
ID · Year · Aspect
art0231 · 2020s (est.) · 400×300

Why it succeeds

  • Recent work (2020s) — Shows that imagining continues to the present day
  • Balanced proportions — Full 400×300 square works equally on all screens
  • Material specificity — "Bicycles" suggests real-world objects, makes installation feel tangible
  • Accessible title — Doesn't require art-historical knowledge to appreciate

What it communicates first

  • Contemporary practice (recent work)
  • Found-object and assemblage thinking
  • A room with real materials
  • Playfulness and exploration

Weaknesses

  • Less formally curated — May read as more casual than museum-quality

Support for principle

Visitor sees living, contemporary imagining. The material specificity (bicycles) makes imagining feel concrete rather than abstract. Recent dating proves the practice continues to now.
