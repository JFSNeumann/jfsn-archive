# EXPERIENCE-STUDIO-PHASE3-CHARTER.md

*Experience Studio Phase Three · Research Program Charter*
*Established: 2026-07-01*

---

## 1. Purpose

Phase Three exists to gather evidence from real visitor encounters with the Archive.

It does not exist to confirm what the Experience Studio already believes. It does not exist to validate the two prototypes currently in place. It does not exist to produce a list of improvements. Its sole purpose is to observe what actually happens when a first-time visitor inhabits the Archive — and to record that faithfully, including what is surprising, discouraging, or inconclusive.

The governing question is unchanged from the study that produced the observation materials:

*When, if at all, does a visitor stop encountering individual objects and begin encountering a lifetime of practice?*

That question will not be answered by further reasoning. It will be answered — partially, provisionally — by observation. Phase Three is where that observation occurs.

---

## 2. Scope

**Phase Three includes:**

- Conducting 3–5 visitor observation sessions using the established protocol.
- Completing an Observation Worksheet and Post-Session Synthesis Form after each session.
- Preserving all session records without editing or interpretation until the full round is complete.
- Analyzing the complete body of evidence once all sessions are finished.
- Identifying patterns, contradictions, and unanswered questions that should govern the next Experience Studio cycle.

**Explicitly out of scope until the first round of observations has been analyzed:**

- Changes to the Archive of any kind, including the two current prototypes.
- New design proposals, however well-reasoned.
- Revisions to the Experience Studio documents.
- New hypotheses developed in the absence of observation evidence.
- Conclusions drawn from a single session or a single visitor's language.

The Archive should remain exactly as it is. The prototypes — removal of the progress bar and an initial page size of 96 works — are the conditions under which the first round of observations will occur. Altering either before analysis would make it impossible to attribute any finding to a specific cause.

---

## 3. Rules of Engagement

**Evidence before change.**
No Archive modification is justified by reasoning alone. Every proposed change must be preceded by evidence from observation. If a proposal cannot be grounded in something a visitor actually did or said, it is not yet ready to be proposed.

**Preserve the current Archive.**
The Archive is the instrument of the study. Adjusting the instrument during the study contaminates the results. Nothing changes until the first round is complete and analyzed.

**Separate observation from interpretation.**
Observation is what happened. Interpretation is what it might mean. The Observation Worksheet holds observations. The Post-Session Synthesis Form holds inferences, clearly labeled as such. These categories must remain distinct throughout the research phase and into analysis.

**Record surprises with equal care as confirmations.**
A visitor who filters immediately and leaves the receiving state before accumulation can begin is as informative as a visitor who dwells in the grid for thirty minutes. A session in which the transformation does not occur is not a failed session. The instinct to weight confirming evidence more heavily than disconfirming evidence is the most common source of bad conclusions. Resist it.

**Do not draw conclusions from a single session.**
One visitor is an anecdote. Three to five visitors, examined together for patterns and contradictions, begin to constitute evidence. No finding from a single session should drive a design decision.

---

## 4. Completion Criteria

The first research cycle is complete when all of the following exist:

- 3–5 completed Observation Worksheets, one per session, with chronological logs covering the full visit.
- 3–5 completed Post-Session Synthesis Forms with observations, quotations, reflections, and inferences kept in separate sections.
- At least two visitor quotations per session — one from during or immediately after the visit, one from the chromatic river question.
- An honest answer to the required question on each Synthesis Form: *What happened in this session that I did not expect?*
- A record of whether and when the reserve question was used across sessions.

Incomplete records should not be used in analysis. A session without a Synthesis Form is an anecdote, not evidence.

---

## 5. Transition to Analysis

When the first round of sessions is complete, the Experience Studio resumes — not to design, but to analyze.

The first task is to lay all Observation Worksheets side by side and look for patterns in behavior before reading the Synthesis Forms. Behavior is more durable evidence than language. Language is more durable evidence than the facilitator's impressions. Work in that order.

The analysis should produce answers — or honest acknowledgments of uncertainty — to the following:

- In which visitors, if any, did the experience of the Archive appear to change in kind rather than in degree? At what point, and under what conditions?
- What did visitors do in the first three minutes? Did early filter use correlate with a different quality of visit?
- How did visitors describe the chromatic river when shown it? What vocabulary did they use — time, navigation, decoration, or something else?
- Where were the surprises? What happened that the Experience Studio did not anticipate?
- What remains unknown after five sessions that was also unknown before them?

If the sessions disagree with each other — the shift appears under one set of conditions in some visitors and is absent under similar conditions in others — the correct response is a sixth and seventh session designed around the disagreement itself, not a majority vote among five accounts and not a default to whichever finding the Studio expected going in. Disagreement at this scale is data about where the next study should look, not noise to be resolved away.

The analysis does not conclude with a list of changes. It concludes with a ranked set of questions — what we now have evidence about, what we remain uncertain about, and what the next Experience Studio cycle should investigate. Changes, if any, follow from that ranking. They do not precede it.

---

## 6. Definition of Success

Phase Three is successful if the first round of observations produces honest, well-documented evidence about what actually happens when a first-time visitor inhabits the Archive.

It is not measured by the number of Archive changes made. It is not measured by whether the transformation was observed in every visitor, or in any visitor. It is not measured by whether the current prototypes are vindicated.

It is measured by whether the Experience Studio returns from the observation phase knowing something it did not know before — and whether that knowledge is grounded in what visitors did and said, rather than in what the Studio expected them to do and say.

A round of sessions in which every visitor filtered immediately, never dwelled in the grid, and described the river as a navigation tool would be a successful round. It would produce the clearest possible evidence that the conditions for the transformation are not yet available to first-time visitors — and that evidence would be more valuable than any number of design sessions conducted in its absence.

The goal of the Experience Studio has always been to protect the conditions under which a visitor can move from encountering objects to encountering an act. Phase Three is where we learn whether those conditions currently exist.

The next work belongs to real visitors.

---

*Phase Three begins with the first session.*
*It ends when the evidence has been gathered and honestly read.*
*Everything else waits.*
