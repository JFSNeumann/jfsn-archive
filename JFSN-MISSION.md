# JFSN Mission

## Why This Archive Exists

JFSN exists to preserve a creative life.

The purpose of this archive is not to sell artwork, build an audience, market a brand, or promote a career.

Its purpose is to preserve the work, stories, ideas, experiments, failures, discoveries, and relationships that shaped a lifetime of making things.

This archive exists so future family members, friends, researchers, collectors, and curious visitors can better understand both the artwork and the person behind it.

If the artwork survives but the story is lost, an important part of the legacy disappears.

JFSN exists to preserve both.

---

## The Story Behind The Work

Jeff attended art school and expected that art might become a larger part of his professional life.

Life followed a different path.

Work, marriage, children, responsibilities, and career demands occupied much of the following decades.

The desire to create never disappeared.

It simply waited.

When family responsibilities eased, Jeff returned to making work with renewed energy and commitment.

The archive documents both the artwork and that return.

Persistence is one of its central themes.

---

## What The Archive Contains

The collection currently contains approximately 1,084 surviving works spanning from the 1970s to the present.

Media include:

- collage
- assemblage (cataloged under sculpture)
- sculpture
- photography
- painting
- mixed media

Many works incorporate found materials, discarded objects, obsolete technology, and everyday items.

Examples include:

- CDs
- floppy disks
- keyboards
- tape
- prism paper
- salvaged materials

These objects were often chosen because they were available, affordable, visually interesting, and seemed to deserve another life.

## Core Themes

Recurring themes throughout the archive include:

- Guernica
- Torsos & Faces
- Mr. SNOWmann
- Crosses
- Assemblage
- Memory
- Loss
- Transformation
- Collaboration
- Reinterpretation
- Reuse

Very few works are truly finished.

Pieces are frequently revised, reconstructed, combined, altered, or transformed into something new.

The process is often as important as the result.

---

## Lost Works

A significant number of works have been lost over the years, including pieces destroyed by water damage.

Some may have been among the strongest works ever created.

Only photographs remain.

One purpose of JFSN is to ensure these works are not lost twice.

Their absence is part of the story.

---

## Family

Family is central to this archive.

Jeff's daughters and grandchildren are part of the creative story.

For several years, grandchildren's drawings have been incorporated into new artwork.

In some cases it becomes difficult to determine where one person's contribution ends and another begins.

This blending of generations is meaningful and intentional.

The archive preserves not only artwork but evidence of creative collaboration across generations.

---

## Preservation Principles

When decisions must be made, prioritize:

1. Preservation
2. Context
3. Storytelling
4. Family history
5. Authenticity
6. Long-term maintainability

Technology should support these goals, not replace them.

Design should serve understanding, not distraction.

Simplicity should be preferred whenever possible.

---

## The Guiding Question

For every future addition, improvement, or change, ask:

"Will this help a future grandchild understand Jeff and his life better?"

If the answer is yes, it is likely worth doing.

If the answer is no, it is probably lower priority.

---

## The Ultimate Goal

The goal is not to build a better art website.

The goal is to preserve a creative life.
